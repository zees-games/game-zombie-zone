# Design

Why game-zombie-zone is shaped the way it is: the decisions behind the rules in `CLAUDE.md`, one section each. Survival maps are in [survival.md](survival.md), the zombies' models and animation in [zombies.md](zombies.md), and bug stories in [history.md](history.md).

The design brief was the genre's own: the four-player co-operative zombie campaigns, the specials that punish a team that splits up, and the big community servers that ran them with twenty-odd players, harder specials, more frequent hordes and a hundred kinds of "party box" that zombies dropped. **Built 2026-10-10 from mg-prop-hunt's skeleton** (module, services, wire, client, HUD, spectate, progress, avatars, figure, vote, map documents and props, renamed `ph` → `zz` and then rewritten wherever they named a disguise, a hunter or a taunt). Where a comment says "mg-wipeout found…" or "another game in this family…", the finding is that game's and the line was kept because it is still true here.

## Decision 1: the zombies are the server's, and a client draws them

dot-npc's reason, which is stronger here than anywhere: a zombie's position is a pathfinder, a steering pass and a crowd pushing on it, none of which is reproducible on two machines. So zombies are **not predicted**. Each one is a `DotNetIdentity` with `ZzNpcNet` (position, yaw, health, state, animation, flags) and is relevant by distance and kind (`ZzNetBridge.make_interest`: commons 80 m, specials 260 m, the brute always; a crowd half sent has holes only at its edge, which the dark and a 3 s linger cover; see survival.md). A client is told a zombie exists (NPC, with its kind and variant so the body is chosen once), interpolates its snapshots, and is told when it is gone and whether it died (NPC_GONE), so a body can fall rather than vanish. `headless_net` spawns one, moves it on the server, and checks the client's copy is within 0.6 m and that its death arrives as a death.

The survivors **are** predicted (dot-player-controller), and everything that changes how a survivor moves is a controller **modifier** (`ZzController`: PINNED, DOWN, LIMP, TAGGED, BUSY, HASTE, LOW_GRAVITY, HEAVY and eight KNOCK directions), because a modifier is replicated with the state and the client applies it on the same tick the server does. A pin or a knock written as a velocity on one end only is a survivor pulled back every tick.

## Decision 2: the zombies' ground is the map's own floor plan

A map document carries `nav.floors` (rectangles), and `ZzNpcs.nav_for` lays dot-npc's point graph over them and cuts out every solid box and every prop taller than half a metre, and every **shut gate**. Opening a gate rebuilds the graph without it (`ZzHorde.open_gate`), which is what lets a barrier hold the horde back and then let it through. zombie-zone-maps generates `nav.floors` from the same regions the walls come from, so the two plans cannot drift; `headless_maps` checks, on every map, that the graph joins every objective, sleeper, spawn hint and the end area to the start, and that the end is cut off while the gates are shut.

**The graph is built off the frame in a hosted game** (`ZzGame.nav_async`, set by the client and the server; a suite leaves it off and gets the graph at once). `ZzNpcs.nav_job` returns a `ZzNavJob`: the strict and loose builds, the reach check and the disk cache as one resumable job, run on a `WorkerThreadPool` task (`zz_nav_threads` tasks; the edge and cover passes split across that many) or, with 0 or no threads, in `zz_nav_budget_ms` slices a tick. The builder touches no physics space, only the document's boxes, so a thread is safe; the props' sizes are read on the main thread first and the document is copied. Until the map's first graph lands the round stays in its ready phase (`ZzHorde.nav_blocks_play`: survivors walk, nothing spawns, the clock waits) and a HUD panel in the bottom-right shows "Navigation regenerating" with a percent; the server tells clients with `ZzEvents.Kind.NAV` (twice a second, only when the percent moves). A gate opening mid-round rebuilds the same way and the old graph serves until the new one lands.

**A mob is only spawned where it can reach somebody** (`ZzHorde._reaches_somebody`, by the graph's connected pieces), and **never inside a safe room** (`_keep_out_of_safe_rooms`). Both were found by `dedicated`: the hold-out's mob spawned behind the barrier the hold-out was opening (`headless_run` arms it: 40 of 40 spawns stranded without the check), and six wanderers stood waiting in the end room. Nothing errored; the stand-ins held the switch with nothing to shoot.

**dot-npc changed for this game**, and its [navigation](../dot-npc/docs/navigation.md) and [history](../dot-npc/docs/history.md) docs have each: bucketed indexes for thousands of points, a binary-heap A*, de-duplication by distance (the old grid key split the graph at doorways and cut the start room off), partial paths that wait instead of repathing every tick (100 ms a tick with a crowd at a shut gate), connected-piece labels so an unreachable goal costs nothing, and `eye_height` (sight was cast from the feet, which start on the floor, so every NPC was blind).

## Decision 3: the director paces, the commons crowd, the specials wait

- **Commons** idle in kinds (stand, sit, feed, mill, shamble) with an alertness drawn per zombie; one that sees a survivor screams, and the scream wakes everything within earshot (`spread_alert`). Only `attackers_per_survivor` (6) take an attack slot on one survivor; the rest take a place on a ring round them, so a crowd surrounds instead of queueing in a line. A mob is a burst spread over a few seconds from points out of sight, ahead of the team along the map's `route` (dot-npc-ai-director's flow) and behind it.
- **Specials** pick a victim by a score (alone, far from the team, already hurt, not already held) and wait up to six seconds for a good moment (`ready_to_strike`): a leaper from cover, a strangler with a clear line, a ram with a straight run. A special that attacks the moment it sees somebody is a special the team kills on sight.
- **The brute** is its own event, with its own music and health scaled by the team.
- **The sleeper** sits until somebody comes too close, then goes for that one person.

Every number is a cvar or a `zz_config.gd` field, and difficulty scales what a hit does (`damage_scale`: 0.35, 1, 2.5, 5) and friendly fire with it.

## Decision 4: down is not dead, and borrowed health is inside the health

At zero health a survivor goes **down** (a pool of 300 that bleeds out, a pistol, a crawl at `zz_down_crawl_speed` m/s, default 1.4, 0 stays put, replicated and followed; it is the DOWN modifier's `max_speed_scale` and `friction_scale` in the predicted motor, set by `ZzController.set_crawl_speed`, because friction left unscaled stalls a slow crawl) up to `max_incaps` times; the next is death. A revive or pills give **borrowed health**, which drains. It is kept **inside** `DotHealth.health` with `temp_health` saying how much of it is borrowed, so dot-combat's damage, hitboxes and replication see one number; a hit takes the borrowed part first (`absorb_temp`), and a medkit replaces it with real health. A dead survivor is rescued beside the team after `rescue_seconds`.

## Decision 5: a box is data, and an outcome is a list of verbs

`boxes/boxes.json` is the whole content: types, each with weighted outcomes, each a title, a message and a list of actions such as `{"do": "airstrike", "target": "nearest", "count": 3, "damage": 60}`. `ZzBoxes.load_text` refuses a file with a verb it does not have (so a typo is a refused file with a reason, not a box that silently does nothing), and `_do_<verb>` is one function per verb. `who` selects opener / team / nearest / random / downed / dead. **Lasting effects stack**: two "double the mobs" are four times, each wearing off on its own clock; the HUD lists them, grouped, by the name of who they are on. `headless_run` runs every outcome of every box once against a live map. The owner is meant to write most of the hundred kinds; the shipped 76 are a start and an example of every verb.

**The framework around the data (2026-10-18).** A box is **carried, then deployed**: picking one up puts it in the hands (`ZzPlayer.box_out`, replicated in bit 9 of `net_carry`; `is_held()` is true while it is out, so the gun is put away and the drawn gun hidden) and `ZzBindings.BOX` (X, `ACT_BOX`) puts it away or takes it out; asking for another weapon slot puts it away and cancels a deploy. First person draws `ZzBoxHands` under the camera (the crate and two `ZeeViewArms`), third person the figure's `show_box`; both pose the shared `ZzBoxCrate` from the replicated deploy fraction (lowered over the first 40 percent, lid open from 55 percent). The old B key is gone; `ACT_DEPLOY` stays on the wire for the stand-ins only. In detail: E picks a box up into the hands (`box_carry` slots, default 1; 0 restores E-opens-on-the-ground), and primary fire (`ZzBindings.FIRE`, Mouse 1 by default, now a rebindable row) held for `box_deploy_seconds` (default 1.0, valid 0.5 to 15, in the server config file like every `ZzConfig` key) opens it at the feet through the same hold mechanism as a revive (`using == &"deploy"`, a meter in the HUD, a ring that shrinks and a pack on the back in the figure). Releasing, walking more than `box_deploy_radius` away, or going down cancels it; a down or death drops what was carried. **`box_labels` is off by default**, so the opening is the surprise (the outcome's title and message are shown, the type never is). Off makes every box look the same: the server sends `unknown` as the loot item (`ZzLoot.shown`) and as the carried-slot index, so the type is never on the wire. Each type carries `polarity`, `impact` (self / radius / everyone / game), `radius`, `stacks`, `max_stacks`, `survives_round` and a `duration` (`instant`, `seconds` drawn uniformly between min and max at open, or `round`); `load_text` refuses a bad value. A **named effect** is an action `{"do": "effect", "name": "x"}` whose handler (a `ZzBoxEffect` subclass in `ZzBoxes.HANDLERS`, or `register_handler`) gets `on_start`, `on_stack`, `on_tick`, `on_end` and `modify_zombie_damage`. The registry is server-side; the existing EFFECTS event carries the HUD strip (stacks and remaining seconds, -1 for "this round"). A non-stacking effect refreshes when opened again; a stacking one counts up to `max_stacks`. `end_round()` (a survival wave cleared, the map ending) ends every game-impact or round-long effect unless it sets `survives_round`. **Speed boxes** (`zz_box_speed.gd`, handlers `move_speed` and `crawl_speed`; boxes swift, marathon, lifeline, long_crawl) give every living survivor a `MOVE_BOOST` (x1.3) or `CRAWL_BOOST` (x1.8) controller modifier for seconds or the round; they are non-stacking (opening again refreshes), a crawl box is not dropped while `zz_down_crawl_speed` is 0 (`ZzBoxEffect.available`), and `ZzPlayer.reset_map` clears both. **Instakill** (`zz_box_instakill.gd`) is the template: positive, game-wide, non-stacking; commons and sleepers die in one hit, specials take `box_instakill_special_scale` times the damage; `box_instakill_min` / `_max` override the file's range.

## Decision 6: a second weapon pack is a list, not a fork

The server carries two packs: zee-dot-weapons and zed-dot-weapons (built for this game, 2026-10-10). zee builds every rig's catalogue, every world model's art and every report from its own tables, so a second pack registers into those (`ZeeWeaponPack.add_source` and friends, added to zee for this) and `ZzWeapons.use_packs` is the one line here that names it, called by the world before any rig is built because a rig reads its catalogue once. `ZzConfig.second_weapon_pack` turns it off. `ZzWeapons` sorts everything into tiers by **tag** (`sidearm`, `primary`, `heavy`, `melee`, `thrown`), so loot, the boxes' "upgrade" and "heavy weapon", and the stand-ins' "is this better" mix both packs without a list.

**What a weapon puts into the world goes through `ZzGame._throw`**, and until it existed nothing did: `_advance_weapons` resolved `outcome.shots` and dropped `outcome.spawns`, so every frag, sticky charge and launcher shell in zee cost ammunition and did nothing. A spawn becomes one of `ZzHazards`' projectiles by its weapon's tags: `molotov` a fire, `pipe_bomb` a beeping lure that goes off, `bile` a jar the horde comes to, anything else a GRENADE on its fuse (resting where it lands), or a ROCKET when it barely drops (flown straight on both ends; the client is told only a start and a velocity). A weapon tagged `incendiary` (the flamethrower) sets what it hits alight (`ZzHorde._on_damage_applied`). `headless_run`'s "both weapon packs, and what they throw" throws each one at commons.


## Decision 7: optional packs are found, not linked in

Weapons and monsters that are not part of the game (zz-modern-weapons, zz-medieval-weapons, zz-historical-monsters) arrive as addons, and the game must run, parse and pass its suites without any of them. `game/zz_packs.gd` scans `res://addons` at start (both ends, `ZzGame._ready`, before `ZzWeapons.use_packs`) for `spawn_provider.gd`, loads it by path and calls it by name, the same pattern as game-playground's hooks. A pack's weapons reach the rigs through zee's `add_source` (a provider's `weapon_defs(types)`, and optionally `weapon_art()`), so tiers, boxes, loot and stand-in kits mix them in with no list; a `weapon` row with no definition behind it is logged and never handed out. A pack's `npc` rows become catalogue entries (`ZzNpcs._pack_defs`, the common's brain unless `spawn.brain` names a script, a built-in zombie body by `spawn.body`) and the director sends them: as specials with a weight against 1.0 per built-in kind, or, if `spawn.boss` or listed under `monsters.boss`, in place of some brutes (`boss_chance`, one boss alive). Everything is the server's; clients only draw the kind. Tuning is `cfg/addons/zombie_zone/*.yml` through dot-core's `DotAddonConfig` (`cfg/addons/zombie_zone/packs.yml` documents the keys). **Getting the packs onto a server** is the server owner's `cfg/packs.yml` (dot-server-deploy; `cfg/addons/dot_server_deploy/packs.yml` once migrated), `games: game-zombie-zone: add: [gamemann/zz-modern-weapons, gamemann/zz-medieval-weapons, gamemann/zz-historical-monsters]`: `./server install-games` pins them, the server mounts them before the game loads and every client downloads them in the same content sync. The game's `game.yml` does not list them, so it runs without. After mounting, `ZzPacks` hands each pack's `*Config` class its `pack_cfg_root` (`DotCloudMounter.cfg_root_of`) and this game's id, so a pack's shipped `cfg/` is the weakest layer, then `cfg/game/game-zombie-zone/`, then `cfg/server/`. Availability is `packs.yml` here: `weapons.weights`, `weapons.tiers`, `weapons.not_in.<box|loot|cache|spawner|wall|crate|trader>`, `monsters.weights`, `monsters.min_wave`, `monsters.boss`; the same keys under `cfg/server/addons/zombie_zone/` win. The trader (a shelf of priced weapons; the default never restocks, a timer comes only from a per-map block), the weapon spawners and crates a map lays down, and wall-buys (supported and documented in the comments of `spawner.yml`, though no shipped map sells one) are dot-weapon's spawner system, configured in `cfg/game/game-zombie-zone/dot_weapon/spawner.yml` (per-map `traders:` and `spawners:` blocks nested under `game:` inside `maps:`; a map document carries its own top-level `traders:` and `spawners:`, read by `game/zz_map_doc.gd`; tier prices in `game.shop.prices`); see [operators](operators.md). `examples/headless_packs` holds it against a fixture provider.

## Decision 7: stand-ins play the map, and do not shoot through you

`ZzBots` drives a survivor with the same commands a person sends, in priority order: a pinned friend, a downed friend, a threat (or a shove when crowded), pills or a medkit, reload, something better on the ground, the objective, then the route. **A stand-in holds its fire when a friend is in the line** (`_friend_in_the_way`): before that, four of them bunched in the start room's doorway shot each other down three times in a minute with friendly fire on. A semi-automatic is fired by pulsing the trigger, or a pistol fires once. They exist to fill a server and to let `headless_run` and `dedicated` play a map; they are not meant to be clever. **How they aim and shoot is dot-bot's `DotBotAim`** (one per stand-in, on `DotBotSettings.of(game)`): the view turns at `bot_turn_rate` (walking too), a new zombie is engaged with `bot_reaction` before the first shot and `bot_aim_error` that settles, and `bot_difficulty` moves them with every other game's bots. Never set `command.yaw` straight onto a target.

## The HUD: dot-ui's glance pieces

`zz_hud.gd` draws health as a `DotVitals` (borrowed health as its `bonus`, white after the real fill; DOWN and DEAD as its `status`, all in the low colour; red at the limp line), the weapon as a `DotAmmoCounter` (an endless reserve as ∞, low at a quarter of a magazine), a hold-out's time left or a survival map's time held as a `DotScoreStrip` clock under the title, the medkit and pills as `DotKeyHints` rows with their bound keys, and confirmed hits as `DotHitMarker` and `DotDamageNumbers`. **The number is real health, not real plus borrowed**: borrowed health drains, and a number that included it would fall while nobody was hitting you; the white length says what is borrowed. **A hit is drawn only from the server's confirmation**: `ZzGame.hit_landed` (a survivor's damage applied to a zombie; never friendly fire, never the world's), sent to the shooter alone as a HIT event, connected straight to the world offline. Zee's red burst on the body comes from the same event (`ZzClient._draw_body_hit`, `ZeeShotFx.play_body_hit` by `has_method` for an older pack), because a zombie has no physics body the presentation ray could meet. The health plate ends 206 pixels off the bottom, above the chat box's full eight-row log (202). Colours from `ZzHud.piece_theme()`; `tools/shot.sh --view=hit --spawn=common:6` renders the marker, a number, borrowed health and a hold-out clock. Naming these classes makes the pack need a client shell with dot-ui API level 4; the derived `requires.json` asks for it and `headless_run` checks it.

### Down the sights

The right mouse button (`ZzBindings.AIM`, "Mouse 2", the family's Aim row) raises zee's view model (`ZeeViewModel.aim`), and `ZzClient._present_aim` zooms the lens by the weapon's `aim_fov_scale` from `base_fov`, which is where the menu's field of view lands (`settings.bind_camera(self, &"base_fov")`), so a zoom never compounds. `ZzHud.set_aim` fades the centre dot with dot-ui's `DotCrosshair.opacity_for_aim` (whole at the hip, gone half way up; a shell older than dot-ui API 5 keeps it) and shows zee's `ZeeScopeOverlay` through a scoped gun, which zee draws without the gun. Nobody aims down, held, in third person, spectating or with the pointer free, and releasing the pointer lets go. **Presentation only**, as in the arena: nothing the server simulates reads it, so spread and damage are the hip's. The shove was on the right mouse button until 2026-10-12 and kept F. `tools/shot.sh --view=ads --out=res://screenshots/ads` frames the hip, the gun coming up and aimed for an iron-sighted gun and a scoped one, with a line per frame of the dot's alpha, the scope and the lens.

## Toys: loose ones only

Every map places a few of dot-props' loose toys (the document's `toys`, `ZzMap._build_toys`): barrels and tyres in the streets, footballs and beach balls where people played, a bowling set in the mall. Never a fixed one (a see-saw, a trampoline): it would stand on the zombies' ground, which the nav floors do not know about, and be a wall a stand-in walks into. Survivors and zombies walk through a loose toy; a survivor's walk sends it on (`ZzGame._advance_toys`; a crowd that shoved barrels would be steering round its own debris), a shot down the same ray pushes it and goes on to the combat (`_shoot_toys`, dot-props' `shot_along` before `resolve_shot`, whose trace never sees a toy, so a barrel is never cover), and a pipe bomb's or a tank's blast throws it (`exploded`). A new map builds them home. Replicated as `net/zz_toy_net.gd` through TOYS after MAP; a client registers every mirror the moment TOYS arrives and hands each its toy when its map is built, because dot-net stops reading a snapshot at the first entity a client does not know. `sv_toys*` are bound REPLICATED and followed.

## Cameras and bodies

From the first play test (survivor bots' faces filling the screen): `mp_solidplayers` (dot-player-controller, 1) puts the player layer in every survivor's motor mask through `DotPlayerBodyRules.apply_to` and gives each body a standing `SolidHull` (`ZzGame._apply_body_mask`, again on every change), so a stand-in stops at your shoulder; `mp_forceview` decides whether F5 may switch to third person. Both bound REPLICATED by the module and followed through the link. The third-person camera is a dot-core `DotCameraBoom` swept from the eye with `CHASE_UP` as `lift`, stopping against `world_mask()`; a `DotCameraFade` on the camera is handed the other survivors and finds zombies on their layer, hiding one at the lens; dot-spectate is handed the level for its chase and death cameras. `tools/camera_check.sh [--render]` flies both cameras round every spawn and route point (armed), walks the player into another with the rule on and off, and draws both views and the walk-in before and after.

## What the first play test changed (2026-10-11)

- **The team list is drawn, not typed.** Its health bars were `▮▯` characters; the HUD's embedded font lacks them and Windows' fallback fonts do too, so every row ended in missing-glyph boxes. Each survivor is a row now (name, a `ColorRect` bar, the number or DOWN / HELD / dead), and `headless_run` fails when any character the game writes is missing from the font.
- **The feed agrees with "You".** `ZzClient.feed_line` writes "You are down (strangler)", "You were picked up"; it read "You is down".
- **Objectives look like what they are** (`game/zz_fixtures.gd`). A gate box may say `"look": "chain_link"` (posts, rails and a see-through alpha-scissor mesh; collision is still the box) and a `use` objective `"prop"`: a padlock and chain hung on the face of the gate it opens (and freed with the gate), a lever, a radio. The first Blackout map's fence lock was a 3.2 m metal box at night, a black slab the camera went nose-first into. Built from primitives because no Kenney kit has a chain-link run or a padlock.
- **A night map has a sky.** The map's own zenith and horizon (0.02 and 0.07) are right for the fog and the fill, which still use them, but drawn they were a black void over every street. `ZzMap._dress_night_sky` draws a deep blue zenith, a lit horizon, seeded stars on the sky's cover and a moon painted where the map's moonlight comes from (the procedural sky's own disc is the light's colour times its energy, which at a moon's 0.09 drew nothing). The cover's equirectangular mapping (`ZzMap.sky_pixel`) was measured from frames: the textbook `atan2(x, -z)` put the moon on the opposite side of the sky.
- **After a wipe the strip counts down.** The map restarted after the 10 s intermission all along, but the screen said "nobody left to watch" and nothing else, and he quit at four seconds. Between a decided map and the next the HUD shows "Trying again" or "Next: <map>" with a clock; ROUND carries the seconds and the next map's id to a client.
- **The HUD follows the menu's theme.** `ZzHud.piece_theme()` is the accent only (the boxes' amber, the health green); plates, words and outline are dot-menu's theme through `follow_hud` (dark by default), and the HUD's Labels set no colour of their own except accent lines (a shout, the boxes' effects).

## What running it found

- **Shots did no damage to zombies**: the hitboxes resolved their owner before the zombie's `DotHealth` existed. Health is created first now, then the hitboxes, then `refresh()`.
- **Idle commons never noticed anybody**: sight from the feet (dot-npc's `eye_height` fix), and the brain overwrote `sight_scale` every tick (it is the character's `alertness` now).
- **The start room had a hole in its side** a stand-in walked out of and fell; the frontage walls close it.
- **The practice street's spawns were inside its tables**, found by `headless_maps`; and zombie-zone-maps' builder stretched door floors along the wrong axis, found the same way.
- **A command and a cvar were both `zz_boxes`**; the cvar is `zz_box_drops`. **Push-to-talk and shove were both V**; shove is F.
- **Three stand-ins called Odell**: names are drawn from the ones not taken.
- **A delivered server always played the practice street first**: the world lays a map out in `_ready`, before the module adds the delivered maps, and `start()` rebuilt that one. It re-picks now when the map it holds is not one the server would choose. And **`zz_server.gd` logged `config.round_seconds`**, a prop-hunt field, as a SCRIPT ERROR no suite here loads that scene to see. Both found by dot-server-deploy's `zombiezone_client`, which joins a real client to the delivered pack.

- **H is help** (dot-menu's stock key; `ZzSettings._fill_help` writes the goal and rules), so nothing in `ZzBindings` may default to H; the medkit moved to G on 2026-10-11 and `headless_run` checks it.
- **M is the map list** (dot-menu's map menu): `ZzGame.map_rows()` and `change_map_now()` (the next map plus dot-match's `begin_round_now`) serve both solo and the server, where dot-vote's map menu lets the owner's `sv_map_menu_flag` change it now and gives anybody else a vote. Nothing in `ZzBindings` may default to M; `headless_run` and `dedicated` check it.
- **Every `ZzEvents.Kind` must fit `ZzEvent.KIND_BITS`** (6 bits, 64 kinds); at 5, HURT and AMMO went out as HELLO and MAP for a day. `headless_net` checks it ([history](docs/history.md)).
- **A brain seeds from `npc.seed`, never `npc.instance_id`** (an object id moves with every allocation); `seed_base` is `config.seed_value`. Suites wait for a zombie's state, never time its seeded choice ([history](docs/history.md)).
- **Your gun is two rigs**: the world's (`ZzPlayer.weapons`, decides) and the client's drawn one (`ZzPlayer.view_rig`, what the HUD counts and the view model plays). Both take `ZzWeapons.command_for` (held hands, an empty magazine reloading itself); offline the buttons go onto the world's sample through `ZzPlayer.command_stamp`, never onto `controller.current_command` (sampled over); the drawn rig is corrected by `correct_view_rig` once quiet. `headless_weapons` presses the client's buttons ([history](docs/history.md)).
- **A shot starting inside a survivor skips them** (dot-combat's `pass_through_sets`); stand-ins test a friend's hitboxes, and `headless_run` measures them over the first round, not at 60 s ([history](docs/history.md)).

## Look, and project.godot

**Surfaces are photographs (2026-10-19).** `ZzMaterials.KIND_OF` names, for each map `mat`, the dot-map surface kind that draws it, one for a wall and one for a floor (`role_of`: a slab 0.6 m or thinner and wider than 1.5 m is a floor, which is how a painted surface is plaster on a wall and clean concrete underfoot); a box may say `"surface": "brick"` to override (a kind or one of `DotMapSurfaces.ALIASES`). `KIND_TINT` scales each photograph to the brightness of the old pastel grid (white tile is 0.97 on average, ground is yellow), and the map's own tint multiplies on top, paled halfway to white. The textures are the kinds listed in `assets/surfaces/kinds.txt`, **copied into this tree by dot-map's `tools/sync-surfaces.sh` and not read from the addon**, because the client shell bundles dot-map and its 12 MB of textures would ride in every game's download; `sides.json` makes them client-only (a server draws nothing). A build without dot-map or without the copy keeps the generated look, kind by kind. Barricade boards keep the generated look: their damage is their tint stepping down, which a photograph under a paled tint would hide. Surfaces with no photograph (carpet, lockers, glass, water, lines, hedges, rubber) are as they were. The non-metal instance of the library draws with `metallic_scale` 0, because a lightmap holds diffuse light only and a baked corrugated roof at 0.5 metallic drew nearly black; metal stays live-lit (`is_shiny`).

**Look**: building surfaces were Kenney's grid under dusted pastels (still the fallback) (`ZzMaterials.PASTELS`, `DUST`), so a dark zombie reads against a pale wall; light is a dot-lighting document from the map's `environment` (`ZzMap.light_document`), the map's own moon or low sun without the rig's 0.35 daylight floor, drawn through dot-lighting's `painted()` (`ZzMap.light_profile`: ambient `AMBIENT` x and fill `FILL` x `ambient_energy`, contrast 1.04 because the grade crushed dark zombies in shade at 1.12, glow only above white). The Fill light is re-aimed after the map turns its sun (`_aim_fill`) and blended with the sun in `set_daylight`, or night would get a day-strength fill. Measured under Compatibility (2026-10-12, school and mall, day and night): the school's sunlit court 3.0 % → 2.5 % blown, its night ground 0.44 → 0.50 mean with nothing crushed, the mall 0.55 → 0.60 by day, 0.39 → 0.42 by night. Night maps stay night; check both renderers with `tools/shot.sh` before changing a number.

Without `maps/` linked in (CI clones this repository alone) `headless_maps` checks the practice street only, and says so.

**`project.godot` carries no comments**: the editor drops them, and any key left at its default, the first time it saves the file, so the reasons for its settings live here. Gravity is left at the engine default and nothing reads it: `ZzConfig.gravity` is written onto the world's own physics space, because a project setting does not travel inside a delivered pack. The renderer is `gl_compatibility` because that is what the delivered client runs, and lighting is tuned under it. The canvas texture filter is Nearest so the prototype grid reads as discrete squares at distance. Texture imports keep `detect_3d/compress_to=0` (and `[importer_defaults]` starts new ones that way) so the editor never re-imports one VRAM-compressed on a first open.

## Delivery and the map vote

The maps are their own repository and pack, [zombie-zone-maps](../zombie-zone-maps): `maps/` here is a dot-bootstrap link, and `game.yml` does not name the pack; the server owner names `gamemann/zombie-zone-maps` (or their own) in their deployment's map config, and `ZzModule` adds every mount's `maps/` to the catalogue at load and after every `zz_reload`. A client is sent the one document being played. No `class_name` anywhere in `game/` or `npcs/`, because a mounted pack cannot register one; the brains are named by path.

**`cfg/maps.yml` says which maps rotate** (dot-map's `DotMapRotationFile`, looked for in the `--config` directory; `sv_map_rotation_file` names it, `zz_maps_reload` re-reads it, `zz_rotation <name>` switches). `ZzCatalogue` holds documents, not `DotMapDef`s, so it keeps a mirror of the ids for the file to mark `in_rotation` on. `rotating()` (the draw, the ballot, nominations) honours the mark; `playable()` does not, so `zz_map <id>` and the map menu still load a map the file leaves out, and the menu marks it "not in rotation". A rotation in the file replaces `zz_shuffle`; `commands:` renames dot-vote's roles and `zz_map`, `zz_maps`, `zz_reload`. With no file nothing changes. Subscribed pack maps (solo) are matched by id only. `dedicated` section "rotation file".

**The map vote** (`game/zz_vote.gd`, MANUAL, applied at a round's end): under MANUAL `ZzVote` opens a passed rock-the-vote itself (dot-vote does not; `rtv_delay_sec` 0), and `_game_load` refreshes the map list after `_add_delivered_maps`. Without either, `!rtv` answers "Rocked. 1 of 1." and nothing opens, or a delivered server's ballot holds only the practice map and never opens. `examples/dedicated` arms both. The match answers dot-vote's `round_in_progress_fn` (`world.match_node.is_live()`, set by name because an older shell's dot-vote lacks it), so a `time_up: finish_round` limit that runs out in warmup or between rounds changes at once instead of holding for `finish_round_max_sec`; `dedicated` checks both halves.

## Blood

`ZzClient.blood` (`game/zz_blood.gd`, a `DotFxBlood` from dot-fx, no autoload) splatters the first world, prop or barricade surface behind a zombie or survivor the local shooter hit: `_on_hit` casts from the hit point away from the camera. The `gore` setting (Video, Image; default on) and `fx_quality` feed `DotFxDecalConfig`; a map change clears it. The shooter splatters from their own HIT; everybody else from the server's SPLAT event (`ZzEvents.Kind.SPLAT`, appended last: a point and a 12-bit octahedral normal, 11 bytes, sent to every ready peer but the shooter, capped at 24 per second server-wide, only within 60 m of the camera; an older client ignores the unknown kind and sees only its own). `headless_look` holds it.

## Sound

Sound follows the map document: each box's `mat` is its collision shape's `surface` meta, so survivors' footsteps go by surface (dot-audio's `DotAudioFootsteps`); every `ceiling` or `roof` box is an indoor ambience zone; the outdoor bed and ground come from `ZzAudio.MAP_SOUND` or the document's `ambience` and `surface`. Over that, `DotAmbience` plays a quiet mood chosen from the map (document `ambient`, `ZzAudio.MAP_MOOD`, or a vote over a converted map's sound names) and crossfades on a change; under a roof (an upward ray from the camera, with hysteresis) the map's `indoor` mood plays instead. Levels are measured with `addons/dot_audio/tools/loudness.sh` and held in the family's −17 LUFS band by `loudness_trim_db`. A dead body's landing is dot-player-char's stock death sounds, in `ZzAudio`'s catalogue and played from the world view's ragdoll yard.

## A hold's day

A survival map fades between its night (the converter's placeholder) and a day. A day the map's own entities derive (`ZzMapDoc.environment_from_world`, `take_derived_light`) is that day, not the default overcast one; a derived night sky replaces the night as before.

## Putting it live

The client shell needs dot-ui API level 4. Tag zee-dot-weapons (`add_source`) and zed, dot-npc (native search, `warm()`, `go_rough`), dot-npc-ai (the shared surround, rough corners), dot-npc-ai-director (API level 2, `DotNpcDirectorWaves`), dot-player-char (API level 3, the skeletal ragdolls) and anything else changed; add `content/zombiezone/` and a client example to dot-server-deploy and rebuild the shell; then the game, and the maps pack after it (its Blackout maps say `exodus`, which an older game refuses).
